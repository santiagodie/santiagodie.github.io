# Design

Visual system of santiagodie.com (v2, 2026-10-17; v2.1 additions 2026-10-18; v2.3, the chat as the About section and hidden empty sections, 2026-10-18; v2.4, the real content, the full About answer and compact project cards, 2026-10-19; v2.5, language flags and the full-screen mobile menu, 2026-10-19). Product context lives in PRODUCT.md.

## Non-negotiable rules

Set by the owner; they win over any recommendation from any design skill. Copied verbatim from the v2 brief (Spanish original). They replace the v1 rules (navy page background).

> - Fondo principal blanco #FFFFFF. Bone #F5F0E1 como fondo secundario para alternar secciones. Navy #0B1F3A para textos, titulares y bloques destacados.
> - Gold #C9A227: solo sobre azul marino, o como detalle decorativo fino (líneas, marcos, iconos) sobre claro. Nunca como color de texto sobre blanco o hueso, porque no tiene contraste suficiente.
> - No se pueden añadir colores nuevos. Logo sin modificar: sd-navy.svg sobre fondos claros y sd-gold.svg sobre azul marino (las versiones de sd-logo\).
> - Prohibido: glassmorphism, neones, degradados de colores, partículas, redes neuronales de fondo, cursores personalizados, scroll-jacking. Respeta prefers-reduced-motion en todo.

Still binding from v1: derived tints (lighter, darker, transparent) of the four colours are allowed, new hues are not; content about the owner is never invented; motion is short, purposeful and respects `prefers-reduced-motion`. Missing content is no longer shown with a TODO marker on the published page (owner's choice, 2026-10-18, replacing the v1 rule):

> Hidden sections (owner's rule, 2026-10-18): paragraphs and entries that still start with `TODO` stay in `i18n/*.json` but are never published. A section left without published content is not published either: not the section, not its link in the navigation (desktop and mobile menu), not its point on the progress line; the visible sections are numbered 01, 02, 03... without gaps and keep alternating left/right and white/bone. A section comes back by itself once it has content. Contact is never empty, because the chat's notice links to it: it always has the real GitHub and LinkedIn profiles and a short translated sentence; the email stays TODO (unpublished) until the owner decides, and the CV button only exists when the PDF does.

Since v2.4 (2026-10-19) every section has the owner's real content, and Contact also publishes the email (contact@santiagodie.com, owner's decision) and the location. Content texts are used exactly as supplied in EN/ES/FR; corrections are proposed to the owner, never applied silently.

v2.1 additions (2026-10-18), also binding. The first one is verbatim from the v2.1 brief:

> - La punta de la línea de progreso: un punto luminoso con un halo dorado suave (drop-shadow o blur del mismo gold con opacidad baja), y detrás una estela que se desvanece en unas decenas de px. Es una excepción a la regla de "sin neones" solo para este elemento: brillo contenido, un solo color, nada de tonos fluorescentes ni degradados multicolor (solo opacidades del gold).

- The About chat animation is presented as "a language model" in general, never as the internals of Claude or of any specific model, and its interface and texts copy no real product. Token ids are real (`cl100k_base`); probabilities, embeddings and attention weights are illustrative. No note says so on the page (removed at the owner's request, v2.3).
- Every animated thing has a complete static form: without JavaScript or with `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, the About chat is shown answered with its message-limit notice (reduced motion: a short fade, no zoom), sections are simply there, and the progress line is complete and still.
- Scroll motion animates `transform`, `opacity` and `stroke-dashoffset` only, plays once per element, never leaves content half-visible, and never holds or hijacks the scroll. Content is visible in the HTML by default; the hidden starting state only exists under `html.js` with motion allowed.

Design direction: a professional 2026 portfolio people remember, still sober. Character comes from the owner's own material (the SD stroke, the tokenizer banner, real project evidence), typography and rhythm, never from generic effects.

## Typography

- **Hubot Sans** (display: name, section titles, card titles), GitHub's display typeface, used wide and heavy for an editorial voice.
- **Mona Sans** (text), its companion text face, for reading.
- **JetBrains Mono** for real data only: metrics, periods, repository names, section numbers, addresses (email, profile links, the CV file) and everything in the About chat (token ids, language codes). About 13 % of the visible text nodes outside the chat (62.7 % before lot 3).
- Metadata that is words, not data, is **Mona Sans**: course names (13px, tertiary navy), technologies on cards and in Experience (13px, weight 500, tertiary navy, gold dot separators), skills (15px, weight 500, secondary navy) and the location in Contact.

## Signature elements

1. **Drawn monogram**: the hero SD (`sd-navy.svg` path, unchanged) draws itself once as one continuous stroke (`stroke-dashoffset`). Reduced motion: shown drawn.
2. **The About chat**: section 02, right after the projects (owner's decision, 2026-11-16: the work comes first), introduces the owner as a chat. Someone asks an AI "Who is Santiago Die?" (translated per page) and the answer is the owner's own About text, three paragraphs starting "Based on my analysis…". Press send and the camera zooms into the message: tokens with their real ids, six-cell embedding vectors, attention arcs and a pulse through four blocks, softmax bars over five candidates, the winner flying into the answer, a flash of the cycle for the next eight tokens, then the rest streamed word by word (about 18 s in all, with small mono stage labels). Then the chat has hit its message limit: a notice with the reset time links to Contact to ask Santiago directly, and the box switches off. All text is in the generated HTML.
   - **Draft, version (b)** (lot 6, kept unpublished by the owner's decision): the question «Santiago, preséntate» with a first-person answer, its tokens and its `llm.json` values in the three languages, in `data/drafts/about-b.json`. The build never reads `data/drafts/`; to publish it, copy its prompt/answer into `chat` in i18n, its tokens into `data/tokens.json` and its values into `data/llm.json`.
3. **Progress line with a trail**: one gold line (1.5px, rounded turns like the monogram) that is drawn as you scroll and retracts when you scroll back. It connects the section numbers, alternating sides on wide screens (01 left, 02 right, ...) and crossing between sections with a rounded monoline turn through the empty band above each heading. Its tip is a small gold point with a soft gold halo and a short fading trail (the one sanctioned glow, see above); the line, its tip and its trail fade in (150ms) only once the drawn stroke is as long as the trail (48px), so no lone gold dot or loose stub shows before the line has started. When the tip reaches a section number, the number lights up and stays lit; the navigation's current section follows the tip. Decorative: `aria-hidden`, `pointer-events: none`. Below 48em it runs straight down the left margin. It starts in the hero: under the actions, a small “Featured” label (Mona Sans semibold, secondary navy, never gold on white) heads the titles of the three featured projects, each a link to its card in 01 Projects (gold underline like “Contact”, 1px at rest, 2px on hover and focus; 44px targets). They hang on the line itself, at its x: three lit nodes joined by a 1.5px gold stroke drawn in CSS (so it is there without JavaScript), and the scrolled line continues from the last node down to 01. The hero index is the map, the section is the proof. Entrance: the monogram starts, the titles settle (200 ms) while the stroke draws down through them (three linear pieces, 450 ms) and each node lights as it is reached (from 90 % and transparent, 200 ms); then the role (650 ms) and the actions (750 ms). Reduced motion and no JavaScript: the stroke and nodes are simply there.
4. **Evidence-first projects**: three featured cards in one row with their README metrics in mono (linked to their README section where it exists); the rest as small cards that each lead straight to the repository (owner's choice in v2.4: no screenshots on the page).

Optional About text under the chat is set as display copy (Hubot Sans 500, up to 2.5rem). Display headings keep tight leading (0.9–1.2) on purpose. Subheads have two levels only: an h3 that titles an item (an entry) is `--step-2`; an h3 that opens a group inside a section is `--step-1`, always in display type, never a small mono label.

## Layout and spacing

- Content max width 76rem; side margins `clamp(1.25rem, 5vw, 4rem)` plus safe-area insets; gutter `clamp(1.25rem, 2.5vw, 2rem)`.
- Section padding `clamp(5rem, 11vw, 9rem)`. Sections alternate white / bone in the order they are shown (the build assigns it, so a hidden section never leaves two of the same in a row); Contact and the footer are navy.
- From 64em: 12-column section grid; titles span the full width, text bodies start at column 3, Projects uses the full width.
- Breakpoints: 22.5em (icon-only menu button), 36em (More projects row: about 2¼ cards in view), 48em (entries, skills and contact in columns), 64em (desktop navigation, hero name beside the monogram, featured cards three across, More projects row: about 3⅓ cards in view). The featured grid uses a fixed column count, so a last card alone keeps its width and sits on the left. The chat's notice switches from one row to two below a 34rem-wide panel (container query).
- Radii: 6px (chips), 8px (buttons), 10–16px (menus, cards).

## Components

- **Header**: sticky, transparent over the hero (navy logo and links); after 16px of scroll it fades to navy (gold logo, bone links, gold active link with a 1.5px gold rule). Below 64em a "Menu" button opens the mobile menu, and over the hero the header's logo is hidden (the hero's monogram is the logo there): invisible and out of the tab order until the header turns navy. Without JavaScript the header never turns navy, so its logo is always shown.
- **Mobile menu** (below 64em with JavaScript; inspired by the menus of apple.com and supersolid.com): a navy panel over the whole screen (100dvh, safe areas included), a modal dialog (`role="dialog"`, `aria-modal`, named "Menu"). Its own bar sits exactly where the header's is: the gold SD on the left, the close button (monoline bone X and "Close", where the menu button was) on the right. Then the sections as a large list in Hubot Sans (bone, `clamp(1.75rem, min(1.1rem + 4vw, 7.5svh), 2.75rem)`), each with its number in small gold JetBrains Mono (the page's numbers, recomputed when a section is hidden), separated by bone hairlines (14 %); the current section in gold. At the bottom: the GitHub and LinkedIn buttons, the languages as a row of flags with their names, and a mono line, "SANTIAGO DIE ©2026". Focus goes to the first link and stays inside (Tab wraps); Escape and the close button return it to the menu button; a section link closes the menu and the page scrolls there. The page behind does not scroll (the scrollbar gutter is always reserved, so nothing jumps), and the progress line and the About chat hold still. Without JavaScript the links simply sit in the header, as before.
- **Social buttons**: 40px square (44px target), 1px border, 8px radius; on hover or focus they round to a circle, lift 2px, fill (navy on light, bone on navy) and show a mono label below. Inspired by Uiverse.io components by akshat-patel28 and EcheverriaJesus (credited in the CSS).
- **Language menu**: on desktop, a menu button showing the current language's flag and an arrow (`aria-label` "Language: English", translated); white popover with the three languages as links, each a flag and its name in its own language, small (`hreflang`, `lang`), the current one underlined in gold. Arrows, Home/End, Enter, Escape, click outside. Switching keeps the current section. It runs as a view transition (lot 9; Chromium 126+ and Safari 18.2+, while Firefox and reduced motion reload as before): the other language's page arrives at the same section, its title at the same height on screen. The header, the monogram and that section's number and title carry across; the rest cross-fades (500 ms). The arriving page does not replay the hero's entrance or the sections' assembly, and Back returns the same way. Hovering the menu button prefetches the other two pages (speculation rules), and a document rule prefetches a language link on hover or press. In the mobile menu and without JavaScript the languages are a plain row of links, flag and name.
- **Flags**: inline SVG drawn once as symbols (United Kingdom 2:1; Spain, the civil flag without the arms, and France 3:2), 14px high, 2px radius, with a 1px hairline edge (navy 20 % on light, bone 32 % on navy) so the white and the yellow keep their shape. Decorative (`aria-hidden`): the link or button names the language. Flags are the one exception to the palette: national flags in their own colours (owner's request, v2.5, 2026-10-19).
- **Language suggestion**: a slim bone bar in the page flow above the header, shown only when the browser prefers another available language; decided by the inline head script before the first paint (no layout shift); dismissal remembered. Dismissed, it gathers up (height, padding and opacity, 500 ms) instead of vanishing, so the page rises rather than jumps, and focus goes to the header's first control in view: the logo, or on phones over the hero, where the logo is hidden, the menu button (lot 9). Below 48em it is a single 44px line with only the link and the close button (44px target); the full sentence is the link's accessible name, starting with the visible words.
- **Name under the pointer** (lot 10): with a fine pointer and motion allowed, each letter of the name grows lighter as the pointer comes near (Hubot Sans from 750, its maximum, down to 500 within 220px; eased back after). The build writes the name letter by letter inside an `aria-hidden` wrapper, with the whole name visually hidden for assistive technology; each letter keeps its resting width (measured, then fixed), so nothing moves (CLS 0). It starts once the name has risen and its font is in; the loop runs only while the pointer is near or letters settle. Not on touch, not with reduced motion.
- **The monogram travels to the header** (lot 10, owner's request, with GSAP's ScrollTrigger; reworked on phones and wide screens after the owner's test): at the top of the page the header has no logo on any width (the hero's monogram is the logo there; the header's logo link still takes keyboard focus and shows then). Its own layer (`.head-band`) sits under the monogram, and the monogram under the header and everything it opens (band 48, monogram 49, header 50): the language menu and the phone menu always cover it.   - **Anchoring when closing**: what was pressed stays still on screen. A summary in view stays where it is and the content below it rises with the curve. The card's button, below its content, stays where it is while the page scrolls by what the fold takes away, in the same frame. "Hide skills" keeps what is below the disclosure still. If the trigger is out of view, what is below stays still and the trigger lands between the header and the bottom of the screen. Native scroll anchoring (`overflow-anchor`, absent in Safari) is off while a fold moves, so only this mechanism acts. Measured: ≤ 0.5 px in every case (3 cases × 4 components × 390/1440 × ES/FR).
  - **Turning back**: a tap mid-way reverses from the current height and opacity, in the same frame, taking the share of the time that is left.
  - **Progress line**: while a fold moves it keeps its geometry (`disclosure:motion`) and measures once when it lands; measuring it every frame cost 6–9 long frames a tap at CPU ×4. ## Motion

- **Time scale** (owner's rule, lot 9): "a press, 0.16 s · a reveal, 0.7 s · a full scene, 1.2 s"; leaving is quicker. The tokens live in `css/styles.css`, and main.js reads them:

  | Token | Value | For |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | `--dur-press` | 160 ms | A response to a tap or a pointer: hover and focus colours, a pressed button, the card lift, the header turning navy, the language menu, the section numbers lighting up, the scroller's arrows |
  | `--dur-reveal` | 700 ms | What arrives: sections assembling, the name rising, card frames tracing; disclosures opening |
  | `--dur-reveal-out` | 500 ms | Disclosures closing, the language suggestion leaving, the cross-fade between languages |
  | `--dur-scene` | 1200 ms | A whole choreography: the hero's entrance, the mobile menu opening |
  | `--dur-scene-out` | 700 ms | The mobile menu closing |

  Off the scale on purpose: the About chat (a demonstration of about 18 s with its own timings) and the progress line (it follows the scroll). Easing: `--ease-out: cubic-bezier(0.23, 1, 0.32, 1)` for UI, `--ease-in-out: cubic-bezier(0.77, 0, 0.175, 1)` for drawn strokes, `--ease-drawer` for the menu panel. "Nothing just appears; everything came from somewhere": what opens grows from its trigger, what closes gathers back into it, and changing language carries the page across (see Language menu).
- Once per visit, a scene of 1.2 s (1.75 s before lot 9): the monogram draws (800 ms from the first frame); the featured titles settle and their stroke draws down through them (done by 825 ms); then the role (from 450 ms) and the actions (from 500 ms) settle in, both reveals of 700 ms, so all is in place at 1.2 s. The name is the page's largest text (its LCP element), so it is visible from the first frame and only rises 10px (700 ms, no delay); the role and the actions fade in as they rise. With reduced motion the hero is still from the first frame, with no fade either (decision, 2026-11-16): review-animations asks for gentler motion rather than none, but here what is immediate wins, since the content is readable at once and the LCP is untouched.
- About chat: the section assembles first (number, title, then the panel); the question types itself (34 ms per character) only once the panel has finished appearing and is at least 35 % on screen, then the send button pulses once. With reduced motion there is no typing and nothing to press: once the panel is 35 % on screen, the question bubble, the answer and the notice simply fade in (no zoom, no stages); the answer is not pushed to the live region then, since nobody asked and the text is readable in place. The run (about 18 s) uses Web Animations on `transform`, `opacity` and `stroke-dashoffset` only, pauses in a hidden tab and finishes itself if scrolled away; Skip and Escape go to the end. After the camera pulls out, eight tokens flash through the cycle (300 ms, each 20 % faster), then the rest streams in word by word (each word fades in over 220 ms, the pace quickening slightly, a 200 ms breath before each paragraph); its length is fixed, so the whole answer is written 5 s after the pull-out in every language. The notice enters just after the answer (opacity and 6px up, 320 ms, `--ease-out`; reduced motion: opacity only). Focus after a keyboard run or Skip goes to the notice's link, then Replay.
- Section assembly, once per element, triggered as it enters: number and node first, title (+80 ms), text (+160 ms), then cards or entries (+70 ms each, at most 6 staggered). Each moves 12px and fades in over 700 ms (a reveal) with `--ease-out`; card frames trace in 700 ms (`stroke-dashoffset`) before their content. Time-based rather than scroll-scrubbed, so nothing can stop half-visible.
- The progress line never competes with the chat: while the chat animation plays, the line holds its length and hides its tip and trail, then catches up.
- Progress line: drawn with `stroke-dashoffset` from the scroll position, through a CSS scroll timeline where supported, otherwise one `requestAnimationFrame` update per scroll frame; geometry recalculated only on resize (ResizeObserver), held while a disclosure moves (see Disclosure motion).
- Menus open slower than they close (language menu 150–220/120 ms) and grow from their trigger.
- Mobile menu, a scene (owner's rule, lot 9): the panel uncovers itself from the top first (`clip-path`, 550 ms, drawer curve `cubic-bezier(0.32, 0.72, 0, 1)`) while the close button's two lines turn from the menu's lines into the X (550 ms). Its rows come in at shrinking gaps (opacity and 10px, 500 ms each, `--ease-out`, delays 250, 335, 412, 483, 547, 605, 657 and 702 ms), all in at 1.2 s. It closes in 700 ms, all at once. Nothing waits for it: the first link has focus at once and every link works from the first frame (a link closes the menu and the page starts scrolling at the same time); Escape closes it at once, without the exit. Transitions, so a quick open-close reverses smoothly. Reduced motion: a fade only (180 ms in, 140 ms out), the X at once.
- Hover motion only under `(hover: hover) and (pointer: fine)`; presses scale to 0.97.
- `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`: no movement at all; the logo is shown drawn, the chat's answer and notice fade in without zoom or stages, sections are visible at once, the progress line is complete and still with every number in its normal state.
